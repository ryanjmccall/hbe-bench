### 🍩 Topological Invariants

<details>
<summary><strong>Genus (g)</strong></summary>

**The Definition:** The number of "holes" in a topological space. A sphere has g=0, a torus (donut) has g=1, a double torus has g=2.
<br>
**The "Root Access" Insight:** It classifies surfaces. You can turn a coffee mug into a donut (both g=1) without tearing, but you can't turn a sphere into a donut.

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Euler Characteristic (χ)</strong></summary>

**The Definition:** A number that describes a topological space's shape regardless of how it is bent. For polyhedra: χ = V - E + F. For surfaces: χ = 2 - 2g.
<br>
**The "Root Access" Insight:** It connects geometry (vertices, edges, faces) to topology (genus). It's a checksum for the shape.

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Topological Invariant</strong></summary>

**The Definition:** A property that remains unchanged under continuous deformations (stretching, bending, twisting) but not tearing or gluing.
<br>
**The "Root Access" Insight:** These are the "fingerprints" of shapes. If two shapes have different invariants, they are fundamentally different.

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Sphere (P=0)</strong></summary>

**The Definition:** A surface with genus 0 (zero holes). Any loop drawn on a sphere can be continuously shrunk to a single point.
<br>
**The "Root Access" Insight:** It is the simplest compact surface. It represents a "trivial" topology in this context.

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Torus (P=1)</strong></summary>

**The Definition:** A surface with genus 1 (one hole), like a donut. There are loops (like one going through the hole) that cannot be shrunk to a point without leaving the surface.
<br>
**The "Root Access" Insight:** The existence of non-contractible loops is what makes it topologically distinct from a sphere.

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Topologically Distinct (P=0 vs P=1)</strong></summary>

**The Definition:** You cannot transform a sphere (P=0) into a torus (P=1) or vice versa without tearing or gluing. They belong to different topological classes.
<br>
**The "Root Access" Insight:** This fundamental difference allows for distinct physical phenomena, like different types of solitons or quantum states, depending on the underlying topology.

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Double Torus (P=2)</strong></summary>

**The Definition:** A surface with genus 2 (two holes). Think of two donuts fused together side-by-side.
<br>
**The "Root Access" Insight:** Higher genus surfaces allow for more complex field configurations and richer topological structures.

</details>
